Today is preproduction. Image and video generation come next.

01 · START WITH THE BRIEF

Keep the story. Own the telling.

Use the story topic and reference video assigned to your team. Keep the topic aligned with that reference while choosing your own scenes, dialogue, pacing and production design. Identify every character who matters to the selected sequence.

  • Watch the reference and note the essential story beats.
  • Choose a sequence that can be told clearly in about 1.5–3 minutes.
  • Shorten a longer source rather than trying to reproduce everything.
  • Avoid a documentary-style string of monologues; let the visuals carry the story.
Write down

Assigned story + reference link, key characters, chosen story beats, and what your version will emphasize.

02 · WRITE AND LOCK

Lock the script first.

Write in WriterDuet, Google Docs or another writing tool. The team can draft directly or use an LLM if time is short, but review the result against the assigned story and approve the screenplay together before shot planning.

Screenplay reviewDoes every scene advance the chosen story? Is dialogue purposeful? Can the audience follow the action through images?

03 · ALIGN THE PRODUCTION TEAM

Get on the same page.

Choose two leads: one for image generation and one for video generation. Have them work together on the shot plan so reference images, camera choices and motion support the same visual direction.

  • Assign screenplay coordination; direction and audio roles can be shared as useful.
  • Choose one hero frame or visual reference to anchor lighting and color.
  • Share the reference with the image-generation lead for related shots.
  • If using shared OpenArt credits, distribute usage fairly.
Optional references

Character sheets can be made before shot division or after the first list. Create character, prop or background sheets only where they improve continuity. Track primary and secondary characters; include background characters only when the story needs them.

04 · PLAN EVERY SHOT

Make each row shootable.

Use the supplied workbook as a structure reference, then create your team’s own shot-by-shot production sheet. Each row should let another teammate make the shot without guessing.

The deck maps the later chain across image, video, voice, music, and sound. Copy the full shot-list and character-sheet prompts from the Playbook.

Mix wide, close-up, rear and three-quarter coverage. Identify the speaker clearly for dialogue shots; plan voiceover where it serves the story. Do not rely on a perfect lip-sync close-up for every scene.

05 · REVIEW THE HANDOFF

Ready to hand it off?

  • The screenplay follows the assigned story and is approved.
  • Every shot has a clear action, camera choice and usable generation prompt.
  • Lighting and color stay consistent; important characters and props have reference placeholders.
  • The image-generation and video-generation leads have reviewed the same plan.
  • Dialogue and voiceover are purposeful, and the selected sequence fits the target runtime.
