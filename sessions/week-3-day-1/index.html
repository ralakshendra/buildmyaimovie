01 · CHARACTER SYSTEM

Lock the hero before the shots

The character sheet becomes the visual source of truth for every later generation.

Walkthrough

  1. Choose an original hero name, personality and visual attitude.
  2. Collect several well-lit photos of yourself from different angles. Keep the raw photos private.
  3. Define the costume silhouette, emblem, materials and two or three main colors.
  4. Generate a landscape character sheet and refine it until the face and costume agree across views.
Look for

The same face, emblem, palette and costume construction in every view.

Common failure

Each pose looks like a different person or redesigns the suit.

Fix it

Reduce competing details, use the clearest face references and repeat the exact costume anchors.

Director note A simpler design that stays consistent will look more professional than a complex design that changes in every shot.

02 · WORLD BUILDING

Make one place feel real

A location sheet gives the teaser a stable stage, even when the camera angle changes.

Walkthrough

  1. Choose one real building or place that matches the hero.
  2. Gather clear references from several angles.
  3. Generate a landscape environment sheet focused on the architecture.
  4. Save the approved sheet as the location reference for later shots.
Look for

Matching materials, landmarks, scale and lighting across the views.

Common failure

Scenery takes over and the building becomes hard to recognize.

Fix it

Ask for simpler framing and make the building the main subject.

Director note The audience only needs enough geography to believe the next shot belongs in the same world.

03 · TITLE ART

Turn the identity into a title

The title should feel designed for this hero and stay readable over moving footage.

Walkthrough

  1. Use the approved character sheet as the visual reference.
  2. Create the hero name or emblem on a plain black background.
  3. Check spelling, letter shape and contrast at a small size.
  4. Remove the background in Adobe Express and export a clean transparent PNG.
Look for

Readable lettering, a clean edge and a style connected to the costume.

Common failure

A black rectangle covers the footage or the name becomes unreadable.

Fix it

Remove the background, simplify the title and test it over a bright and dark frame.

Director note A title is part of the movie's visual language. It should feel related to the hero, not pasted on afterward.

04 · A-ROLL

Give every main shot a job

The demonstrated workflow uses text plus the assigned character and location references.

Walkthrough

  1. Plan three jobs: establish the world, reveal the hero and show one action beat.
  2. Attach the correct character sheet and hero base to each relevant generation.
  3. Describe one clear camera idea and one clear action per shot.
  4. Generate alternate takes with the same direction when you need more editing choices.
Look for

The right hero, the right place and a readable action in each take.

Common failure

The hero drifts, the location changes or too many actions compete in one clip.

Fix it

Confirm the assigned references and simplify the action. Do not use start and end frames for this demonstrated method.

Director note Alternate takes are useful only when they preserve the same story purpose.
05 · B-ROLL + SOUND

Add detail without breaking continuity

B-roll supports the main moments with a title animation, close detail or short atmosphere beat.

Walkthrough

  1. Identify what the A-roll still needs: identity, scale, texture or transition.
  2. Create one or two supporting shots using the same hero and location system.
  3. Keep the frame shape consistent with the rest of the teaser.
  4. Review sound effects and choose background music you own or have permission to use.
Look for

Details that strengthen the hero reveal and make cuts feel intentional.

Common failure

A flashy insert introduces a new suit, place or visual style.

Fix it

Return to the approved references and remove any insert that distracts from the core reveal.

Director note Sound can make a small visual moment feel large. Give each effect a reason to exist.
06 · EDIT

Shape the reveal in Filmora

The strongest order lets the audience understand the world, meet the hero and feel the action.

Walkthrough

  1. Create and save the Filmora project before arranging clips.
  2. Import the approved A-roll, B-roll, transparent title and audio.
  3. Build a short sequence. The edit can be nonlinear when the meaning stays clear.
  4. Place the title above the footage, trim dead frames and remove black gaps.
  5. Balance sound effects and score, then watch the full timeline without stopping.
Look for

A clean opening, clear hero reveal, decisive action beat and readable title.

Common failure

The edit feels like unrelated clips or the sound arrives late.

Fix it

Cut around the reveal, align sound to visible events and remove any shot without a clear job.

Director note A short teaser benefits from confidence. Enter each shot late and leave before its energy drops.
WATCH THE EXPORT · CHECK EVERY FRAME
07 · EXPORT REVIEW

Watch the file you will share

The timeline is not the deliverable. The exported teaser is.

Walkthrough

  1. Export with the settings available in your workflow.
  2. Open the exported file and watch it from the first frame to the last.
  3. Check likeness, costume, location, title, black gaps, audio sync and ending.
  4. Submit the finished teaser to the cohort group by the end of the session day.
  5. Share publicly only when the audio is owned or licensed. Never post the raw face references.
Look for

A complete file that plays smoothly and represents the intended final cut.

Common failure

The project looked correct in the editor, but the export contains a gap or missing layer.

Fix it

Correct the timeline, export again and repeat the complete viewing check.

BEFORE YOU SHARE

Export the film, not your references.

Keep every raw face photo private. Share only the reviewed teaser and audio you can publish.
