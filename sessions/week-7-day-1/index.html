PREPARE

Bring a clear reference of yourself.

Use what you already made.

Bring the three or four photos from different angles that you prepared in the previous session. If you already have a clean portrait, keep it ready too.

For Google Flow

Prepare a clean, front-facing portrait with a simple background. The instructor says busy background details can confuse image-based character creation.

For the video-trained avatar

Use footage with clear light, a visible face and mouth, and measured hand gestures. Keep your hands from crossing your face.

Use your own likeness or footage you have permission to use. The video-trained workflow includes consent and identity checks in the tool.

01 · IMAGE-BASED CHARACTER

Save a character and attach its voice.

Keep the identity input simple.

In a new Google Flow project, use Characters and upload the clean portrait. The class demonstration selected Nano Banana Pro, not Nano Banana 2.

  1. Open Characters, upload the portrait, and choose the class image model.
  2. Choose a template voice. Enter sample dialogue to preview it, then adjust speed or tempo if useful.
  3. Select Add to character so the voice stays with that character in later generations.
  4. Name the character. A character description is optional. The instructor skipped body creation for this workflow.
  5. In video prompts, tag the saved character so the model has a clear identity reference.

Voice limitation: This Flow workflow offered template voices; it did not provide an option to upload a custom voice recording. If preview playback fails, add sample dialogue and refresh before deciding the voice is broken.

Expected result: A saved image-based character with one attached template voice and a recognizable face.

02 · AGENT VIDEO

Review the plan before spending credits.

Let the agent break the ad into scenes.

Turn on Agent in the prompt box. The agent can draft separate scene prompts and ask for approval before it generates.

  1. Set the video approval choice to Always so you can inspect the scene breakdown and credit request before it runs.
  2. In the class demo, set the video model to Omni Flash, the frame to 9:16 portrait, and variations to one.
  3. Tag your character in the prompt. Review each proposed scene for identity, costume, setting, spoken line, and unnecessary text.
  4. Ask the agent to correct any scene that changes the face, voice, or outfit before approving generation.

Setting change: The class found that “a real film set” could suggest real actors in the background. The instructor changed it to a film warehouse with equipment and no actors. Review for the same issue before generation.

Caption check: The instructor warned that generated captions can be inaccurate. Remove them and add captions in an editor or dedicated captioning tool if needed.

03 · VIDEO-TRAINED AVATAR

Give the avatar useful training footage.

Check light, face, and hands.

Soft, even light helps the tool see your expressions and lip movement. Keep gestures below the face and avoid blocking your mouth.

  1. Open the HeyGen avatar workflow using the instructor-shared link. Choose the option to clone a real person.
  2. Upload footage or record with a webcam or phone. The reference guide recommends two to three minutes of continuous speech; follow any current in-tool limits.
  3. Keep your face and mouth visible and your gestures modest. Give the avatar a name and create it.
  4. Choose the voice from the training video or record a voice sample. Complete the consent and identity verification flow for your own likeness.
  5. Generate a short test with dialogue you can review for voice, lip movement, and timing.

Class-era plan note: The recording showed a 15-second free option using Avatar 3. Plans and model choices change, so check what the tool currently offers before relying on those limits. You do not need to buy a plan to complete the comparison if a free test is available.

Expected result: A verified avatar and a short test video with readable speech and natural framing.

04 · REVIEW & CHOOSE

Compare the outputs before you choose one.

Google Flow

Starts from an image and a selected template voice. The instructor described this image-based approach as still developing, with likeness variation and language quality limits.

Check: Does the tagged face stay recognizable? Does the attached voice remain consistent?

Video-trained avatar

Uses your training footage and a consent/identity verification step. The instructor recommended this route for more reliable identity and voice matching.

Check: Do face, voice, lip movement, and frame shape match the footage and your goal?

  • Listen for repeated or cut-off words.
  • Check face, hair, outfit, and proportions in every scene.
  • Look for stretched framing and unexpected background people.
  • Remove unreliable generated text before you decide whether the shot works.

Name the mismatch first.

Before rerunning a generation, say what visibly failed: face, outfit, voice, wording, framing, or background. Then change that one part and compare again.

TROUBLESHOOTING

Make one useful correction at a time.

The Flow character looks unlike you

Use a clean portrait from the multi-angle photos. Remove distracting background objects. Start a new project if old generations are mixing with the new character.

The voice preview is silent

Enter sample dialogue and preview. If playback still fails, refresh and try again. Flow did not accept uploaded audio in the class workflow.

The face changes between scenes

Review each scene prompt and make sure the saved character is tagged. Check whether one scene adds a new face, age, hair, or outfit detail.

The avatar face looks stretched

Check the source and output aspect ratios. Ask the avatar agent to identify the likely cause before changing the framing.

Words or captions look wrong

Check the dialogue for repeated or cut-off words. Remove generated captions and add accurate captions during editing.

TAKE IT INTO PRACTICE

Save both tests and your comparison.

Keep the source photos, generated portraits, characters, and test videos organized in Drive. Record their links, your review, and the version you want to carry into the portfolio session in the Homework Doc through the playbook.
